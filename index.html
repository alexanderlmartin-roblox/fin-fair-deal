Nursing Homes Support Scheme · Ireland

Fin Fair Deal

What a nursing home would actually cost Dad, Mum and the family.

Fair Deal is the Irish State's Nursing Homes Support Scheme. The HSE sets a fixed price with every approved home, Dad pays a means‑tested weekly contribution, and the HSE pays the balance. His contribution is the same whichever approved home he chooses. The HSE's letter of 4 November 2026 has assessed that contribution. This page works out what it means for Mum's cash flow, the savings and the house.

The letter is not the loan decision. If the Nursing Home Loan is approved, the €667.07 house line is paid by the HSE and charged against the house instead, so the weekly cash bill falls to €738.77. The house line runs for 36 months at most (11.25% of value for a couple), then stops whether or not the loan is taken.

Savings

The letter assessed roughly €93,200 of cash. If the balance is now €70,000, a review can be requested 12 months after the assessment; the cash line would fall to about zero. Worth €15 a week, so not urgent.

The €2,000 fee

Confirm with the home that €2,000 is its agreed Fair Deal (NTPF) price and not a private‑payer rate. Dublin agreed rates on the HSE list top out around €1,508. A home cannot charge a Fair Deal resident a top‑up on the care fee itself.

The €450 extras

€450 a week is far above typical social programme and sundry charges. Ask for an itemised list in writing. If some of it is in effect a top‑up on care, it is not permitted under the scheme and the Nursing Homes Support Office (045 920000) will advise.

How the scheme works

Three steps, all through the local Nursing Homes Support Office

1 · Care needs

Does he need long‑term care?

A HSE healthcare professional assesses whether nursing home care is the right option. No money is discussed at this stage.

2 · Financial assessment

What can he contribute?

Income and assets are declared. The office works out a weekly contribution using the 80% / 7.5% formula. A couple is assessed on half of combined means.

3 · Choose a home

Any approved home, same contribution

Public, voluntary or private. The HSE pays the home the balance of its agreed price every week. Optional: the Nursing Home Loan defers the home‑based part until after death.

The rules that change the number

Disregard

The first €36,000 of assets (€72,000 for a couple) is ignored. It is taken off cash assets first, then off property. With €36,000 or less in savings, the whole disregard lands on the house.

3‑year cap

The family home, the proceeds if it is sold, and a qualifying farm or business are charged for 3 years at most: 22.5% of value for a single person, 11.25% for a couple. For the home this is automatic. Time already spent in care counts.

A farm or business only gets the cap if a family successor (18+, a relative) commits to running it for 6 years and it was actively run for 3 of the last 5 years. Apply on part 6 of the form.

5‑year look‑back

Anything given away or transferred in the 5 years before the first application, or at any time after it, is assessed as if he still owned it. Gifting the house now does not take it out of the calculation.

Couples

Married couples and partners of 3+ years are assessed on combined means and pay half the rates: 40% of combined income, 3.75% of combined assets. The spouse at home is not asked to sell up.

Renting the home

If the house is rented out while he is in care, all of that rent can be exempted from the assessment on application (Principal Private Residence Rental Income form, with the RTB registration letter, lease and Revenue notice of assessment). Rent from any other property is assessed at 80%.

Reviews

Either side can ask for a financial review 12 months after the last assessment. If savings fall as he pays, a review lowers the contribution. Any asset sale, including the home, must be reported within 10 working days.

Ceiling

The contribution can never exceed the agreed price of the home. Above that, he is simply self‑funding and Fair Deal pays nothing.

Nursing Home Loan

Also called Ancillary State Support

What it is

Optional. The HSE pays the home‑based (and other property‑based) part of the contribution on his behalf each week and registers a charge on the property. Nothing has to be sold while he is alive. Apply on part 5 of the form, with Fair Deal or later.

Repayment

Repaid to the Revenue Commissioners by his estate. For deaths on or after 5 September 2026 the estate has 18 months; interest runs from the date of death if it is later than that. Deaths before that date had 12 months.

If the house is sold or transferred while he is still in care, repayment is due within 6 months of the sale.

Indexation

The amount owed is uplifted by Consumer Price Index inflation from when each payment was made. No other interest applies if repaid on time.

Deferral

Repayment can be deferred while a spouse or partner still lives in the house. A "connected person" can also apply: a child under 21, a child or sibling with assets under €36,000, a relative on a disability or non‑contributory pension, or a carer who looked after him before he went into care. The house must have been his only residence for 3+ years.

Who should take it

Anyone who is asset‑rich and cash‑poor. It converts a weekly bill into a one‑off charge on the estate capped at 22.5% of the home's value. If there is enough cash to pay the asset part comfortably, skipping it keeps things simpler and avoids the CPI uplift.

What private nursing homes charge, by county

HSE/NTPF agreed maximum weekly prices for private and voluntary homes, July 2026 list (411 homes)

Public HSE units average about €1,955 a week on a different costing basis. Under Fair Deal the price of the home does not change Dad's contribution; it changes how much the HSE tops up. The price matters only if he self‑funds, or for the extras below.

Not covered by Fair Deal

  • Social programme or activities charges, typically billed weekly by private homes.
  • Hairdressing, chiropody, newspapers, outings and personal items.
  • Some therapies (physiotherapy, occupational therapy) in private homes.
  • Short‑term respite or convalescent stays. Fair Deal is for long‑term care only.
  • Prescription charges and GP visits, though a medical card continues to apply.

Ask each shortlisted home for its full list of additional charges in writing before signing the contract. Budget a few hundred euro a month on top of the Fair Deal contribution.
